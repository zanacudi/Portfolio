Comparative Assessment of Expansive Soil Stabilization by Commercially Available Polymers

An interactive companion to Taher, Scalia & Bareither (2020), Transportation Geotechnics, 24, 100387. doi.org/10.1016/j.trgeo.2020.100387

What this paper actually does

Expansive clays shrink and swell with moisture changes, damaging roads and light structures. Departments of Transportation mostly reach for lime or fly ash to fix this. This study asked a simple, practical question: are commercially available liquid polymer soil stabilizers a viable alternative? A highly expansive test soil (75.8% liquid limit, 58.1% plasticity index) was treated with 3% quicklime, 15% Class C fly ash, and 5% of a selected polymer (P4), then compared on four measurements: swelling potential, swelling pressure, unconfined compressive strength, and hydraulic conductivity.

The short answer: polymer was a much weaker structural stabilizer than lime or fly ash, but a far better hydraulic barrier — especially when sprayed on as a coating rather than mixed in bulk. There is no single "best" treatment; the right choice depends on what a project actually needs. The tool below lets you explore that trade-off directly using the paper's own measured numbers.

About this tool. Every number shown above (swell potential, swell pressure, UCS, hydraulic conductivity, LL/PL/PI, curing comparison, and the coating results) is taken directly from Tables 1–2 and Figures 2 and 6 of the published paper. The only thing that is not from the paper is the priority-weighting score in Section 4, which is a simple illustrative composite (weighted average of normalized swell-reduction, strength, and permeability scores) built to let you explore the real data's trade-offs — it is not a design or specification tool.
