Concrete Formwork Design Calculator

An interactive rebuild of the design software from Ibrahim, Ali & Taher (2011–2012), Concrete Formwork Design, University of Sulaimani Faculty of Engineering — a senior capstone project. Chapter 6 of the report describes an 8,000-line Visual Basic 6 desktop application the team built to run these exact calculations; this page reimplements that same logic (Table 4.1 span equations, plyform & lumber property tables) so you can drive it live with sliders.

What "the app" actually does

Fresh concrete pushes on its formwork like a fluid. The formwork's job is to hold that pressure without excessive bending, shear, or deflection until the concrete cures. This report works through the standard cascading design used throughout the concrete formwork industry: sheathing (the panel touching the concrete) is supported by joists, which are supported by stringers, which sit on shores — each stage's allowable span becomes the next stage's tributary load. Walls and columns follow the same cascade with plyform sheathing → studs → wales → ties instead, driven by a lateral pressure that depends on how fast concrete is poured and its temperature (ACI 347).

Each span is governed by whichever of three checks is most restrictive — bending, shear, or deflection — per the project's Table 4.1 equations, reduced by a 95% safety factor. Pick a tab below and drag the sliders; every number is computed live using the same coefficients as the original VB6 app.

About this tool. The gravity-load formulas (deck/joist/stringer/shore) and their coefficients (40.7, 1.11, 73.8, 100, 3.16, 1.67), the ACI 347 lateral pressure formula, and every lumber section property (area, section modulus, moment of inertia) are taken directly from the project's Table 4.1 and Table 4.2 and verified against the report's own worked examples — each formula below reproduces the report's numeric answers to the millimetre. The one exception: the plyform stiffness (EI) values transcribed in the original VB6 source appear to contain some data-entry errors (non-monotonic with thickness), so deflection for plyform sheathing uses a standard-practice modulus of elasticity per plyform class instead, clearly an approximation rather than the report's exact (likely miskeyed) figures.

Source: Ibrahim, R. O., Ali, S. A., & Taher, Z. J. (2011–2012). Concrete Formwork Design. Engineering Projects, Faculty of Engineering, University of Sulaimani. Supervisor: Akram Saleh.
