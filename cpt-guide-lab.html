Guide to Cone Penetration Testing — Interactive Companion

Interactive companion to Robertson, P.K. & Cabal, K. (2022), Guide to Cone Penetration Testing, 7th Edition, Gregg Drilling LLC. Complements the CPT interpretation in the Jefferies & Been series, Module 2 with Robertson's own direct correlations — several of which turn out to agree closely, which is a useful cross-check either way.

About this module

Where the Jefferies & Been modules build a state-parameter-based inversion from first principles (cavity expansion theory, calibrated to laboratory CSL data), this guide is the industry's standard field reference: direct, mostly empirical correlations from a huge base of calibration-chamber and case-history data, built around one central quantity — the soil behaviour–type index, Ic. This module works through the same raw CPT channels (qt, fs, stresses) into Ic, state parameter, friction angle, and several everyday design correlations (OCR, relative density, undrained strength) — all using the guide's own published equations.

1. From qt, fs to the soil behaviour–type index, Ic

The guide's central simplification: instead of reading soil type off a two-axis chart, collapse the normalized tip resistance and friction ratio into a single index whose contours are (approximately) circles on the Qt–Fr chart:

Qt = (qt − σvo) / σ′vo    Fr = (fs / (qt − σvo)) × 100%
Ic = √[(3.47 − log Qt)² + (log Fr + 1.22)²]

This is the exact same formula used as "Robertson & Wride (1998)" in Module 2 — the guide is where that formula actually comes from and continues to be maintained.

2. State parameter and friction angle — a second, independent route

Robertson (2010) fit a direct relationship between ψ and the clean-sand-equivalent normalized resistance, Qtn,cs — no CSL, no λ, no calibration chamber inversion coefficients, just one curve fit to case-history and calibration-chamber data:

ψ = 0.56 − 0.33·log(Qtn,cs)

Jefferies & Been's own strong ψ–friction-angle link then converts this directly to a peak friction angle. Substituting the ψ formula above into φ′=φ′cv−48ψ gives a form written entirely in terms of the CPT reading — and these two forms are algebraically identical (checked directly below), which is exactly the kind of internal-consistency check this whole site has been built around:

φ′ = φ′cv − 48ψ   ≡   φ′ = φ′cv + 15.84·log(Qtn,cs) − 26.88

For comparison, Kulhawy & Mayne's (1990) independent, purely empirical fit for clean quartz sands:

φ′ = 17.6 + 11·log(Qtn)

3. Everyday CPT correlations: OCR, relative density, undrained strength

Uses the same qt, fs, σ′vo from Section 1's sliders above.

Overconsolidation ratio

OCR = 0.25·(Qt)1.25   (Robertson, 2009)    OCR ≈ k·Qt, k≈0.33   (Kulhawy & Mayne, 1990, valid Qt<20)

Relative density (clean sand, Ic < 1.6)

Dr² = Qtn / 350   (simplified for young, uncemented quartz sand; Qtn here uses a fixed stress exponent n=0.5)

Undrained shear strength & sensitivity (fine-grained soil)

su = (qt − σvo) / Nkt    Nkt = 10.5 + 7·log(Fr)   (Robertson, 2012)    St ≈ 7/Fr
About this tool. Section 1's Ic formula and SBTn zone boundaries are transcribed exactly from the guide (Fig. 23). Section 2's ψ–Qtn,cs and Kulhawy–Mayne friction-angle formulas are exact; the algebraic identity shown between the two φ′ forms was verified independently (substituting one equation into the other reproduces the guide's own stated combined form to within rounding) rather than assumed. Qtn,cs is entered directly via slider rather than derived from raw qt/fs, because the full clean-sand-equivalent conversion requires an iterative stress-exponent procedure (the guide's Fig. 48) that is referenced but not fully reproduced in the extracted text used to build this page. Section 3's Dr calculator uses a fixed stress exponent n=0.5 rather than the guide's full Ic-dependent iterative n — a reasonable simplification specifically in the clean-sand regime (Ic<1.6) the formula itself is stated for, but disclosed here rather than left implicit. The Robertson–Campanella (1983) cavity-expansion friction-angle formula is mentioned in the guide but was not implemented here, since its extracted text was too garbled to transcribe with confidence.
