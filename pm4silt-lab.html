PM4Silt — Interactive Companion

Interactive companion to Boulanger, R.W. & Ziotopoulou, K. (2018), PM4Silt (Version 1): A Silt Plasticity Model for Earthquake Engineering Applications, Report No. UCD/CGM-18-01, Center for Geotechnical Modeling, UC Davis. The low-plasticity-silt/clay sibling of PM4Sand — same bounding-surface framework, but positioned from undrained shear strength rather than relative density, and using the classic Been & Jefferies state parameter ψ from the Jefferies & Been modules directly, rather than PM4Sand's relative-density-based ξR.

About this module

PM4Silt shares PM4Sand's bounding-surface architecture and much of its equation structure, but is built for fine-grained soils where relative density is meaningless and undrained shear strength is the quantity engineers actually have data for. The critical state line is positioned internally so that the model reproduces a user-specified undrained shear strength at critical state, su,cs,eq, rather than being built up from emax/emin and DR. Because of this, PM4Silt uses the state parameter ψ (Been & Jefferies 1985) — the exact same quantity used throughout the Jefferies & Been critical-state modules on this site — instead of PM4Sand's relative state parameter index ξR. This module covers how su,cs,eq and ψ relate, how the bounding/dilatancy surfaces are built from ψ, and how the model's primary parameters are calibrated in practice.

1. Undrained shear strength: earthquake-rate input, post-shaking output

Because su is rate-dependent, the model separates the strength used during strong shaking from the (lower) strength used to check post-shaking static stability:

su,cs,static = Fsu · su,cs,eq

su,cs,eq can also be entered as a strength ratio su,cs,eq/σ′vc, computed internally from σ′vc at model initialization. Fsu defaults to 1.0 and is normally only set (<1.0) at the end of strong shaking, to account for rate effects and cyclic degradation/remolding when checking post-earthquake stability.

2. Elastic shear modulus — steeper stress-dependence than sand

G = Go·pA·(p′/pA)nG

Same form as PM4Sand's G(p′), but with an adjustable exponent nG — default 0.75 for silt, versus PM4Sand's fixed 0.50. The higher exponent means silt stiffness rises more steeply with confining stress than sand's.

3. Bounding & dilatancy surfaces vs. state parameter ψ

Md = M·exp(nd·ψ)     Mb,wet = M·exp(−nb,wet·ψ)   (loose of critical, ψ≥0)

On the dense ("dry") side, ψ<0, Mb instead follows a curved interpolation from M at critical state up to a maximum Mb,max=2·sinφmax as p′→0 — the report states this relationship in words clearly, but the exact interpolation function could not be transcribed from the extracted PDF text with confidence, so only its two verified anchor points (M at ψ=0, and Mb,max at the origin) are shown below as a dashed qualitative sketch. Defaults: nd=0.30, nb,wet=0.80, nb,dry=0.50, φ′cv=32°.

4. Estimating a target CRR, and calibrating hpo

The report gives an explicit, quotable rule of thumb linking strength ratio to cyclic strength at earthquake loading rates: the cyclic stress ratio to cause 3% peak shear strain in 30 uniform cycles is about 55–70% of su,cs,eq/σ′vc (equivalently 70–90% of the slower su,cs,static/σ′vc). hpo is then adjusted by trial simulation — last, after Go and su,cs,eq are fixed — until the model's cyclic response falls in this target range.

CRR30,3% ≈ (0.55 to 0.70) × su,cs,eq/σ′vc
Uses the strength ratio from Section 1 above. There is no closed-form for hpo itself — only for this target range it must be calibrated against.

About this tool. The su,cs,static=Fsu·su,cs,eq relationship, the G(p′) form with nG, the Md and wet-side Mb exponential forms, the 55–70% CRR rule-of-thumb, and the zmax/Cε piecewise default formulas are all transcribed directly from the report's Sections 2.2–2.3 and 4.1 (verified against the raw extracted text span-by-span, including the non-obvious non-monotonic zmax=10/40/20 breakpoints). The dense-side (\"dry\") bounding-surface interpolation (Eqs 15–16 of the report) involves a stacked fraction that the PDF text extraction garbled beyond confident reconstruction, so it is shown only qualitatively (dashed, anchored at its two verified endpoints) rather than as an exact curve — the same standard applied throughout this site: transcribe exactly, or disclose and approximate, never guess silently. This module does not implement the full incremental plastic stress-strain integration; treat it as a notation and calibration-logic guide, not a substitute for running the model.
