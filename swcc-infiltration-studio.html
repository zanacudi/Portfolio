Transient boundary conditions

Properties ready for validation.

Sign convention: depth z is positive downward; pressure head h is negative under suction; downward Darcy flux is positive. The update is θᵢⁿ⁺¹ = θᵢⁿ + Δt(qᵢ₋½ − qᵢ₊½)/Δz, with face conductivities taken as the harmonic mean of the two cells — the series-resistance result for flow across layering.

The solver. Time stepping is implicit (backward Euler) and the nonlinearity is handled by the modified Picard iteration of Celia, Bouloutas & Zarba (1990) on the mixed form, solved as a tridiagonal system. That is the scheme HYDRUS-1D uses, and it is mass conservative by construction, because storage is written as a change in θ rather than as C·dh. The step size is chosen by the solver and shrunk when the iteration struggles. You set the nodes per layer, from 1 to 60; an explicit scheme could not offer that choice, because its stability limit Δt < Δz²C/2K makes ten times the nodes cost a hundred times the steps.

What it is checked against. Press Verify solver in the editor above. Two of the four cases have answers that are known exactly: sealed at the top with the base held at zero pressure head, the only steady state is no flow at all, so h = z − L everywhere; and under a steady flux over a freely draining base the profile settles at unit gradient, so K(h) = q in every cell. The page reports the error it actually made on both, with a mass audit and a refinement check on three grids.

What it still is not. Flow is one-dimensional and downward, the soil does not change volume, and hysteresis between wetting and drying is not represented. Conductivity is floored at an effective saturation of 0.01, so each soil has a lowest conductivity it can produce. For design, use a benchmarked code.

1. Mixed-Form 1D Richards' Partial Differential Equation (Mode A — Active):

In Mode A (Richards' PDE), we explicitly solve the 1D vertical unsaturated Darcy–Buckingham PDE using a Cell-Centered Finite Volume Method (FVM) across N control volumes, with implicit backward-Euler time stepping and modified-Picard iteration on the nonlinearity. N is the number of layers times the nodes per layer, which you set in the editor, so the grid is yours to refine:

Governing PDE:   ∂θ(ψ) / ∂t = ∂/∂z [ K(Se) (∂ψ/∂z − 1) ] = −∂q / ∂z
FVM Discretization (Cell i):   θin+1 = θin + (Δt / Δz) · ( qi−1/2n − qi+1/2n )
Interfacial Darcy Flux:   qi−1/2 = Ki−1/2 · [ (ψi−1 − ψi) / Δz + 1 ]

How SWCC & K(Se) Close the PDE: At every time step, each cell's volumetric water content θi is mapped via monotonic PCHIP spline inversion of its 6-point SWCC to obtain matric head ψi = −|ψi|, and unsaturated conductivity is evaluated via the Mualem–van Genuchten relation K(Se) = Ks · Se0.5 [1 − (1 − Se1/m)m]2.

1. Reading the Live State on the SWCC

The horizontal axis is Matric Suction |ψ| (log scale, cm H2O) and the vertical axis is Volumetric Water Content θ. Dry soil starts on the right (high suction pulling tightly on scarce water). As water infiltrates, saturation rises, suction drops, and the live state dot travels leftward and upward along the SWCC toward saturation (θs at |ψ| = 0.1 cm).

2. Click & Drag the 6 Control Nodes

The 6 white control circles sit at suction heads |ψ| = 0.1, 1, 10, 100, 1,000, and 10,000 cm. Dragging nodes upward increases pore water storage capacity (θs − θr) and scales effective saturated conductivity Ks. Monotonic PCHIP spline interpolation ensures physically realistic, non-oscillating retention curves.

3. Why Domain Size (1 vs. 2 vs. 10 Layers) Matters

With 1 Layer, infiltration is governed purely by a single curve's storage capacity and Ks. With 2 Layers, contrasting SWCCs create sharp interfacial phenomena like capillary barriers (fine over coarse) or perched retardation (coarse over fine). With up to 10 Layers, you model realistic field-scale stratified vadose zones.
